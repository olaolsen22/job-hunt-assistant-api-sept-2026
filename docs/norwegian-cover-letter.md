# Cover letters in Norway — formatting, structure, content flow and writing style

Researched **15 October 2026**. Written in English, with Norwegian terms and illustrative wording. Companion to [the Norwegian CV guide](norwegian-cv-formatting.md).

**Layout revision, 15 October 2026:** the guide now uses Telemark's published Norwegian application letters as its traditional letter-layout reference, rather than treating an individually designed contact block as a Norwegian default. Tekna's font, margin and length recommendations remain attributed to Tekna. Exact spacing, typography and the added telephone/email lines are implementation choices. The sample already contained a letter date; this revision moves it to the upper right. CV birth dates and cover-letter dates are different fields. [C6, C8]

## 1. Scope and evidence

This guide covers ordinary employment applications, including software developer roles. It compares Norwegian public services and universities with advice from Norwegian professional associations, employers and named recruitment practitioners. It does not claim to cover every source or establish a compulsory national standard.

**Public guidance:** NAV/Arbeidsplassen, NTNU, NMBU, Karriereveiledning.no, ung.no and county career centres. **Industry guidance:** Tekna, KPMG Norway and named people interviewed by Kode24. Industry advice is not government policy, even when an interviewee works for NAV. Multiple pages from one institution are not independent votes.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Shared guidance:** agreement between reviewed sources.
- **Source-specific advice:** an explicit recommendation from a named publisher.
- **Suggested implementation:** our practical layout or writing choice, not an official rule.
- **Not established:** the reviewed material does not settle the question.

## 2. What the letter should achieve

A jobbsøknad or søknadsbrev should give the employer a reason to interview you. NAV emphasizes answering the actual advertisement, making relevant strengths clear and supporting them with examples. A short, targeted letter is preferable to a general account of your life. [C1]

Karriereveiledning.no frames the purpose around why you want the job, what you can contribute and how you can carry out its tasks. It explicitly acknowledges different employer expectations: there is no single correct style for every organization. [C5]

**Working distinction:** the CV records experience; the letter selects evidence and explains its relevance to this particular employer. Tekna explicitly says the two should complement each other rather than be copies. [C6]

### Traditional Norwegian letter layout: comparison with a published example

Telemark's two application-letter examples show the sender's name, street address, postcode and town at the upper left, with place/date on the right alongside the sender block. Recipient name, organization and postal address follow on the left, before the subject heading. Its separate CV example uses labels including `Tlf.:`, `E-post:` and `Adresse:`. These demonstrate actual published Norwegian examples, not compulsory national rules. [C8, pp. 4–6]

The earlier sample combined a compact CV-style contact block (`Bosted: Oslo, Norge`) with a letter layout. It was readable, but it did not closely reproduce this traditional example. Telephone/email labels are not themselves errors. A town-only location is a compact alternative, not equivalent to a postal address.

| Element | Published example or guidance | Implementation in the revised sample |
|---|---|---|
| Page | Telemark shows a conventional letter; Tekna recommends at most one page. | A4 portrait, one page. |
| Sender | Telemark's letters show name, street address, postcode and town. | Name and address placeholders at upper left. Telephone/email are added for convenience; this is an adaptation. |
| Letter date | Telemark places town/date at the upper right, alongside the sender block. | `Oslo, 15. oktober 2026` at upper right. |
| Recipient | Telemark shows contact person, organization and full postal address. Tekna also recommends recipient details for paper/PDF letters. | Organization and address placeholders; use a named contact only when supplied. |
| Subject | Both Telemark's examples and Tekna identify the position. | Clear, bold position title; reference number if supplied. |
| Font | Tekna lists Arial, Calibri, Georgia and Times New Roman, at 10–12 pt. | Arial, 11 pt body and 12 pt bold subject. Individual assignments are design choices. |
| Margins | Tekna recommends 2.54 cm; allows smaller top/bottom margins if needed. | 2.54 cm on all sides. |
| Spacing | Tekna asks for separation between blocks without prescribing point values. | 14 pt body line spacing and 10 pt after paragraphs; optional design choices. |
| Body | Relevant motivation, qualifications and examples, then a brief ending. | Four paragraphs in this example; change their number and length to fit the content. |
| Sign-off | Tekna accepts `Vennlig hilsen` and a typed name for electronic submission. | `Vennlig hilsen`, followed by the name. |

**Date distinction:** a cover-letter date records when the letter was written. `Født:` in Telemark's CV example records a birth date. Its presence in a CV example does not establish a requirement to include it in every CV, or in a cover letter. The original sample did contain `Oslo, 15. oktober 2026`, but placed it below the recipient block. [C8, pp. 4–6]

### How to adapt this layout

Use genuine sender and recipient details. The revised fictional sample uses bracketed postal-address placeholders so the traditional structure is visible without inventing an address. Replace or remove every placeholder before submitting. Omit an unknown contact person rather than inventing one. For an uploaded PDF, a compact contact header is also possible; Tekna explicitly allows contact information below the signature in a portal/email context. A full postal layout is one option, not a requirement for every online application. [C6]

Do not treat each contact field's own line, `Bosted:`, a particular name size, or a fixed block order as a national standard. For a domestic letter, adding `Norge` after a Norwegian town is an optional contextual choice. Keep the result readable and follow the employer's instructions.

Finished example: the revised fictional developer cover letter PDF (kept outside the repo; `CoverLetterPdfRenderer` implements its layout). The applicant, employer, vacancy, experience and results are fictional; postal-address fields are placeholders. This sample demonstrates the traditional layout adapted with telephone/email lines, not a universal Norwegian template.

## 4. A practical content flow

NMBU describes an introduction, main body and ending: specific motivation, evidence that you can handle the tasks, and a brief conclusion. It also encourages straightforward, natural language. [C3]

The following four-paragraph plan is a **suggested implementation** of that structure. The paragraph labels are drafting aids; they do not need to appear in the submitted letter.

| Part | Reader's question | What to include |
|---|---|---|
| Subject line | Which vacancy? | Exact title; reference number when supplied. |
| 1. Motivation and fit | Why this role here? | A specific task, product or mission that interests you, connected to relevant experience. |
| 2. Main evidence | Can you do the work? | One strong example: the problem, your action, result and relevance. |
| 3. Working style or second strength | How would you contribute? | Evidence of collaboration, judgment, learning or another important requirement. |
| 4. Close | What happens next? | Brief interest in discussing your contribution; useful availability information if relevant. |

### Flexible paragraph lengths and openings

Tekna suggests approximately 300–350 words in 3–4 short paragraphs; NTNU says 3–4 paragraphs are sufficient. Treat these as length guidance, not a minimum or a requirement to allocate a set number of words to each paragraph. The former 100–125-word targets for two evidence paragraphs have been removed: they were editorial choices without evidence of an advantage. Tekna recommends keeping individual examples to at most three sentences. [C2, C6]

The four-part plan above is a drafting option. NTNU's examples also demonstrate opening directly with relevant experience and results, as well as enthusiasm, a relevant conversation, or employer news. Starting with an explicit motivation paragraph is therefore not the only supported approach. Choose the strongest truthful opening for the vacancy. [C2, “Eksempler – Innledning”]

Use enough evidence to make your contribution credible, without repeating the same point. Combine or split paragraphs for readability. Extra paragraph headings and bullets are optional layout decisions; Telemark itself provides both prose and a requirement-led example. [C8, pp. 4–5]

Østfold recommends getting to motivation early, explaining what is interesting about the job rather than merely calling it interesting, and supporting suitability with concrete examples. Its separate structure article cautions against simply repeating the CV. [C7, C9]

### Opening: make motivation specific

**Illustrative wording, not a source quotation:**

Generic: “Jeg søker fordi stillingen virker spennende og gir gode utviklingsmuligheter.”

More specific: “Jeg ønsker å jobbe med bestillingsløsningen deres fordi jeg liker å forbedre tjenester som brukes hver dag. Erfaringen min med API-er og feilhåndtering vil være relevant for integrasjonene dere beskriver.”

The second version names the work and connects it to a contribution. Only use such wording when the vacancy actually describes that work and you have that experience.

### Main evidence: explain how experience becomes useful

Suggested drafting formula: **relevant need → what I did → outcome → why that helps here**.

Fictional example: “I min nåværende rolle forbedret jeg databasespørringene bak et ordre-API. Median svartid gikk fra 420 til 180 ms. Det er erfaring jeg kan bruke i arbeidet med ytelse og stabilitet hos dere.”

Choose evidence you can explain at interview. An outcome can be a reliable release, simpler process or successful collaboration; a percentage is not required. Never invent measurements to make the letter appear stronger.

### Closing: brief and positive

NAV recommends a short ending covering contact, possible start date and interest in an interview. Include availability when useful and accurate; do not guess your notice period. [C1]

Suggested wording: “Jeg utdyper gjerne hvordan erfaringen min kan bidra i teamet deres. Jeg kan tiltre etter avtale.”

Tekna explicitly recommends `Vennlig hilsen` or similar conventional sign-offs followed by your name, and says a typed name is sufficient for email/upload. A handwritten-signature image is therefore unnecessary for the electronic sample. [C6]

## 5. Writing style and tone

ung.no recommends simple, clear language in your own words and reading the letter aloud to see whether it sounds like you. This is a useful editing test for both new graduates and experienced applicants. [C4]

NTNU advises precise wording, removing filler and using concrete examples tied to the vacancy. It also asks for a specific, personal letter informed by research into the employer. [C2]

| Prefer | Avoid | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A concrete contribution | Unsupported superlatives | Gives the reader evidence to assess. |
| Short, direct sentences | Inflated, abstract phrasing | Makes the argument easier to follow. |
| Your own actions | Only describing what the team/company did | Shows what you personally contributed. |
| A specific reason for applying | Praise that fits any employer | Shows attention to this vacancy. |
| A relevant example of collaboration | A list of personality adjectives | Explains behavior rather than asserting it. |

This table is an editorial synthesis, not a list of banned Norwegian words. There is no evidence here that an employer automatically rejects a particular adjective.

**Confidence and personality:** do not undersell relevant achievements, but avoid declaring yourself the perfect candidate without support. Personal writing can mean describing how you work and what interests you; it need not mean disclosing private family or health information. Match the employer's tone without copying its marketing language. These are suggested editorial choices within the sources' broader emphasis on relevance.

## 6. Differences and tensions between sources

- **How to start:** Østfold's structure article suggests identifying where you found the vacancy, while Tekna encourages skipping obvious information and getting to relevant details. Suggested resolution: put title/reference in the heading; mention the source of the vacancy only when requested or useful. [C9, C6]
- **How formal:** Karriereveiledning.no says expectations vary. A restrained professional tone is a practical starting point, not a claim that every Norwegian workplace prefers the same voice. [C5]
- **AI assistance:** ung.no favors writing primarily yourself. In a 2026 Tekna interview, recruiter Sverre Haugen encourages using AI as a thinking partner while retaining ownership of the text. Both approaches leave the applicant responsible for the actual claims and wording. [C4, C10]
- **Paragraphs versus bullets:** a short prose letter is a workable default; Telemark's alternative example shows that requirements can also be addressed with bullets. Choose for clarity and follow the employer's instructions. [C8]

## 7. Software developer applications

KPMG Norway's recruiters emphasize concrete experience and the connection between a candidate's background, motivation and the role; its advice covers graduate hiring, including technology, rather than only software developers. [C11]

Suggested application of the general guidance to developer roles:

1. Identify the actual engineering need: for example integration reliability, accessibility, delivery speed or maintainability.
2. Pick one or two relevant examples; explain your contribution and the outcome. Let the CV carry the full technology inventory.
3. Show how you work with others through a concrete example: clarifying requirements, reviewing code, responding to incidents or testing releases.
4. For a junior applicant, use a genuine course, thesis or personal project when it is your strongest evidence. Label its context accurately.
5. Relate foreign experience to the work you can do. There is no need to retell every international move or duplicate all CV dates.

These are our developer-focused drafting suggestions. They are not requirements imposed by KPMG or a universal Norwegian software-hiring template. NTNU's guidance itself includes examples connecting web-development experience to a role. [C2]

### Avoid generic AI polish

Kode24 reported in November 2025 that NAV department head Monica Hurley received over 140 very similar applications and criticized inflated, repetitive wording. This is a named recruiter's experience, not proof of an AI detector or a national prohibition. [C12]

Haugen's 2026 Tekna interview recommends concentrating on the two or three most important points rather than mechanically responding to every requirement. His broader screening claims are practitioner observations, not a technical specification for all ATS products. [C10]

Practical editing check: remove generic praise and claims that do not help explain your fit. A truthful sentence about your experience can legitimately recur in several applications. Tailor the selection of evidence and its connection to this vacancy, rather than forcing every sentence to be unique.

## 8. PDF letter, email and application portals

Tekna distinguishes traditional paper/PDF formatting from digital contexts: employer details/date suit a letter, while contact details can sit under the signature in email or portal submission. Its advice is contextual, not a reason to invent a contact person or postal address. [C6]

Telemark's electronic-application guidance says limited text boxes call for direct writing, omitting redundant name/address/date introductions and boilerplate about where you found the vacancy. It also stresses completing system fields alongside requested attachments. [C8, p. 10]

| Submission context | Suggested implementation |
|---|---|
| Attached PDF letter | Contact information, employer/recipient details where known, date, clear heading, body and sign-off. |
| General motivation text box | Use the same argument in plain text; omit a large postal-address block. Respect the character limit. |
| Separate application questions | Answer each actual question. A four-paragraph letter is not a substitute for requested answers. |
| CV-only advertisement | Follow the stated process; a cover letter is not universally required. |

For PDF checks, confirm the text is selectable and copies in a sensible order. Plain text, one column and ordinary fonts are practical precautions, not a compatibility certification. No Norwegian cover-letter-specific ATS font, metadata or keyword-density rule was established by this review. Do not add hidden keywords or treat technical metadata as a substitute for visible evidence.

## 9. Drafting and review workflow

This is a suggested process based on the findings above:

1. Save the advertisement and note the two or three most important needs.
2. For each need, write down genuine supporting experience and a possible contribution.
3. Draft a short letter around the strongest evidence; use the four-part plan only if it helps.
4. Check that the opening explains interest in this employer, not only personal career benefits.
5. Remove CV repetition, generic praise and unsupported claims.
6. Read aloud; simplify sentences you would not naturally say.
7. Check employer name, title, dates, contact details and any reference number.
8. Follow the actual submission instructions and preview the final PDF or portal text.

NMBU explicitly recommends checking reference numbers and the spelling of employer/contact names, having someone review the application, and adapting each submission. [C3]
